Stage 0

Scope: where the boundary of "tokenizer research" sits

done

The brief says "you decide where the boundary of the topic sits; tell us how you drew it". Every later number depends on this line.

In scope: five topics

  • Subword algorithms for text language models: BPE, WordPiece, Unigram, SentencePiece.
  • Byte-level, character-level and tokenizer-free models.
  • Foundational NMT-era subword papers.
  • Effects of tokenization: multilingual fertility and fairness, arithmetic, code, reasoning, vocabulary size and scaling.
  • Vocabulary adaptation and transfer between languages or domains.

Out of scope: other senses of "token"

  • Tokenizers for other modalities: VQ image tokens, audio codecs, motion, molecules.
  • Token pruning, merging and reduction; KV-cache eviction.
  • Crypto assets and asset tokenisation.
  • Mixed-modality papers stay only when text tokenization is a central contribution; the judge decides those.

Decisions

The boundary is a list of topics plus named exclusions, written into configADR 0009

The five topics and the exclusions live in config/scope.toml, and every keyword query is tagged with the topic it serves, so coverage per topic can be read off the config. The judge's prompt restates the same boundary in words.

Why: the brief asks how the line was drawn, and a reviewer can only check a line that is written down in one place. Read ADR 0009.

The judge asks for a "substantive contribution or finding", not "mainly about"ADR 0007

A paper that studies tokenization among other things is in. A paper that merely uses a tokenizer is out. This is the question the model scores, with the five topics listed in the prompt.

Why: "mainly about" would drop the effects papers (arithmetic, fairness) that the brief names explicitly. Read ADR 0007.

The corpus keeps only papersADR 0040

Journal, conference and workshop papers and preprints. Package manuals, proceedings front matter, textbooks and theses are excluded even when they are about tokenization, using the type fields the sources record and a page-count rule.

Why: the parse audit found six CRAN manuals, a 188-page textbook and several theses that the judge had let in because they are "about" tokenization. Read ADR 0040.

Where it lives: config/scope.toml. Changing it changes every later number.

Stage 1

Discover: how the data was found

done

Find as many Candidates as possible from open sources, remember how each one was found, and store every raw answer so the run can be replayed offline.

Input
19 keyword queries (6 arXiv, 12 Semantic Scholar, 1 OpenAlex) and 21 hand-picked Seed papers, each with a written reason.
Output
26,386 Candidate Editions with title, authors, year, venue, identifiers, links, licence and the list of Discovery channels that found each one. Every source response saved in the Snapshot.

Nouns born here

Candidate
Any record found during discovery, before it has a verdict.
Seed
A landmark Work used as a starting point for citation expansion. 21 of them, from Sennrich's BPE paper (2015) to Byte Latent Transformer (2024).
Discovery channel
The route a Candidate came by: a named query on a source, or one citation hop (references or citers) from a named Seed. One Edition can have twenty.
Snapshot
The committed record of every source response, keyed by the request. Replaying it rebuilds the manifest byte for byte with no network.

How the four routes differ

  • arXiv search API: title and abstract queries restricted to cs.CL, cs.LG and cs.AI, 500 per page, one request every 3 s. Each Edition then gets its full record (every Version, comments, licence) from arXiv's OAI-PMH feed.
  • Semantic Scholar bulk search: 12 phrase queries by topic. Also serves the citation graph for Seed expansion. No API key, so long throttles are waited out.
  • OpenAlex: one phrase query, kept as a switchable channel. It reaches venues the others miss and lists open-access PDF locations.
  • ACL Anthology: no query API. Each ACL id found elsewhere gets a venue Edition of its own from the paper page, so a preprint and its conference paper stay two rows.

Decisions

Commit the Snapshot of every raw source responseADR 0003

Each response is one gzip file keyed by a hash of the request. Collection reads the Snapshot first and only calls the network on a miss. Keys travel in headers, never in the key, so no credential can land in git.

Why: sources change their answers over time; without the Snapshot a reviewer rerunning months later could not check the counts. Read ADR 0003.

Seeds and one hop of citation expansion, kept distinguishable from keyword searchADR 0009

21 Seeds, each with a one-line reason. For each, its references and its citers are fetched as Candidates and tagged with the Seed. The report shows what expansion adds on its own.

Why: papers that say "digit splitting" or "patches" never match a tokenizer query but cite the landmarks. Read ADR 0009.

Every arXiv Edition carries its full Version history and licenceADR 0006, 0010

One OAI-PMH record per arXiv paper gives every Version with its date, the comments (often the only place a venue is named) and the licence. Fetched only for Editions that pass the rules, to save 5–7 hours of rate-limited calls on rows the rules discard.

Why: the duplicates work needs Version history, and licences must be shown to be respected. ADR 0006, ADR 0010.

A preprint and its venue paper are two Editions, never mergedADR 0008, 0012, 0017

Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex merge a paper's copies into one record. The pipeline splits them: the arXiv id makes the preprint row, the ACL id makes a venue row read from the Anthology paper page. An ACL id that turns out to be a whole proceedings volume counts as no paper.

Why: the near-duplicate stage must have real version and venue duplicates to detect and measure. ADR 0008, ADR 0012, ADR 0017.

OpenAlex added as a switchable channel, then kept on with evidenceADR 0014, 0019

The bar was 20 included Editions found by nothing else, or 20 included venue papers whose only PDF source it gives. It found 332 and 204 respectively on the first full run.

Why: another source costs paging time and Snapshot bytes, so it had to earn its place. ADR 0014, ADR 0019.

Rate limits and throttles are respected, and long ones waited outADR 0004, 0016

arXiv: 3 s between requests with backoff, pages sorted by submission date ascending so earlier pages stay stable. Semantic Scholar without a key: backoff up to 5 minutes, 16 attempts, after three crashed full runs.

Why: the brief asks that rate limits and licences be respected, and the first full run proved the shared pool throttles for longer than six minutes. ADR 0004, ADR 0016.

What could be wrong
  • One arXiv paper sits under two Edition ids, one with an arXiv: prefix, and a CRAN package's open-access link resolved to another paper's PDF. Identifiers from some sources are not normalised before Editions are combined. Issue 44.
  • Coverage is bounded by the query set and 21 Seeds; a sub-topic with its own vocabulary and no Seed is reached only by luck.

Reproduce: just collect from a clone replays the Snapshot offline and rewrites the manifest and data/report.md byte for byte.

Stage 2

Judge relevance: how each Candidate got its verdict

done

Decide, for every Candidate, whether it belongs in the corpus, record why, and measure how good that decision is against hand labels.

Input
26,386 Candidates with title and abstract.
Output
A Relevance verdict per Edition: included (2,705) or excluded, with the stage that decided and the reason, in the manifest.

Nouns born here

Relevance verdict
Included or excluded, plus the stage (rules, withdrawn, not-a-paper, judge) and a reason a reviewer can read.
Audit set
180 hand-labelled Candidates used to choose and measure the verdict, never to train it. 60 from each stratum: rule-rejected, judge-excluded, judge-included.

The five gates, in order

  • Rules (deterministic, free). A title with a strong on-topic term skips the exclusions. Otherwise an exclusion pattern on the title rejects with its name. Then title or abstract must contain a required term (tokeniz…, subword, byte-pair, WordPiece, SentencePiece, vocabulary, byte-level, character-level).
  • Withdrawn: an arXiv paper whose latest Version is a withdrawal is excluded; the bot must not cite retracted results.
  • Not a paper: manuals, proceedings front matter, textbooks and theses, by source type and page count.
  • Judge: a local decision model reads title and abstract and returns P(Yes) to the scope question. Included at P(Yes) ≥ 0.8. Every score is cached in the Snapshot, so replays need no model.
  • Borrowed file: after the judge, an Edition whose only file came from an open-access link and is byte-identical to another Edition's, with no identifier or title linking the two, is excluded; the link resolved to someone else's paper.

How it is checked

The Audit set is a stratified sample, weighted back to the 26,388 so precision and recall describe the population. Rules alone reach precision 0.33 at recall 1.00: they throw nothing relevant away but let through two off-topic papers for every relevant one. The judge is chosen on the 120 rule passes of that set.

Decisions

A rule pre-filter that names the pattern that firedADR 0005

Case-insensitive regexes in config: a keep guard on the title, named exclusion patterns on the title, and a required-term group on title or abstract. Exclusions run first so the recorded reason is the specific off-topic sense, not the generic "missing term".

Why: the judge is the expensive step, and a reviewer must see why each Candidate was rejected. Read ADR 0005.

A local open-weight judge that returns a probability, not a generated answerADR 0007

One forward pass; P(Yes) is the softmax over the Yes and No logits. Nothing is sampled or parsed. Verdicts are cached by a hash of the question, model and input.

Why: a reviewer can rerun collection with no GPU or API key, and a probability lets the threshold be tuned on the Audit set. Read ADR 0007.

Audit set: stratified, weighted, labels drafted blind by agents and checked by the developerADR 0011, 0020

Three agents labelled a third each, seeing only title, abstract and link, never the system's own verdict. 37 hard rows were flagged and all checked by the developer; 48 of 180 were developer-checked in total, 31 labels changed from the draft. The file records who set each label.

Why: a uniform sample would hold almost no hard cases near the judge's boundary, and labels anchored on the system's answer would flatter it. ADR 0011, ADR 0020.

Tev1 4B on Ollama 0.40.5 at threshold 0.8, pinned with its runtimeADR 0021, 0025, 0018

A decision model asked one true/false question through Ollama. The model digest and the Ollama version are pinned together because another runtime changes the scores. An abstract is cut only when the model refuses it as too long, so cached verdicts stay valid. The MPS cache is released every batch so a 7,000-row run fits in 32 GB.

Why: best F1 by a margin of 6 points over the next model; reproducibility demands the exact runtime. ADR 0021, ADR 0025, ADR 0018.

Withdrawn preprints and non-papers are excluded before the judgeADR 0037, 0040

A withdrawal is read from the arXiv record (latest Version at 0 kB). Non-papers are read from source type fields and page counts, after the parse audit found the judge admits anything "about" tokenization.

Why: a grounded bot must not cite retracted results or package manuals. ADR 0037, ADR 0040.

What could be wrong
  • Precision 0.81 means roughly one included paper in five is off topic by the Audit set's reading; recall 0.87 means about one relevant rule-pass in eight is lost.
  • The judge sees only title and abstract. Some sources put body text in the abstract field; those inputs are cut to fit and judged on their start.
  • The Audit set is 180 rows, so the figures above carry sampling error of several points.

Reproduce: just collect replays cached verdicts; just collect --no-judge leaves them pending. The judge itself needs Ollama 0.40.5 exactly.

Stage 3

Acquire and parse: from an included Edition to Chunks

done

Download exactly the files the corpus is pinned to, turn each into clean Section-aware Chunks that can be cited by paper, Section and page, and keep garbled parses out.

Input
2,705 included Editions.
Output
2,690 pinned PDF files (hashes in the Snapshot, files never committed) and 141,348 Chunks in a local parse cache, plus a committed parse report with one line per file.

Nouns born here

Pinned file
For a preprint: the latest Version at Snapshot time, plus v1 when they differ. For an ACL paper: the Anthology PDF. Otherwise the first open-access PDF OpenAlex lists. Each with its sha256.
Section
A headed division of the body as the authors numbered it; an appendix is a Section marked as one.
Chunk
Up to 350 tokens of one Section of one file, never crossing a Section. Kind: text, table or caption. Carries its Edition id, pinned id and Version.
Context header
Title and Section path stored apart from the text, so the embedding sees "WECHSEL › 5 Results › 5.3 Is freezing necessary?" and citations quote clean text.
Citation anchor
Pinned Edition id with Version, Section number and title, page range.
Quality gate
A file with a GROBID error or timeout, fewer than 2 Sections, or under 2,000 characters of Section text gets no Chunks.

What happens to each element

  • Body paragraphs become text Chunks, packed whole until the 350-token budget.
  • Tables become table Chunks: a header line naming table, Section and caption, then one line per row with cells separated by bars. GROBID finds about half of all tables; the cells of a missed one stay inside text Chunks and are counted as "mostly numeric".
  • Figure captions become caption Chunks, since findings are often stated only there.
  • Equations are linearised into the text.
  • Reference lists are kept as a structured bibliography for the duplicates work but never indexed; they match every keyword.
  • Acknowledgements are dropped. Appendices are flagged.
  • Headings GROBID missed (left inside a paragraph in larger type) are recovered, so Related Work is not cited as the Introduction.

How it is checked

A full-corpus parse audit sampled 37 files (25 outliers, 12 random) and compared Chunks with the PDF page by page, then counted each pattern it found across the corpus. Parsed text covers a median 77% of each PDF's characters; the gap is mostly reference lists, which are meant to be absent. Four files were found to be "not a paper" and led to the rule above. Report: 2026-10-18 parse audit.

Decisions

Fetch pins exact Versions and records hashes; PDFs stay out of gitADR 0015, 0002

A separate fetch step downloads the files of every included row, writes each sha256 into the Snapshot's full-text record, and checks re-downloads against it so upstream drift is told apart from pipeline failure.

Why: the duplicates work needs real v1, latest and venue copies, and the brief forbids committing PDFs. ADR 0015, ADR 0002.

GROBID stays the parser after a spike against DoclingADR 0024, 0023

On the 34 Seed PDFs, Docling found 94% of tables against GROBID's 46% and kept more headings, but was 7.3× slower on one GPU, projected at about 30 hours for the corpus against 35 minutes for GROBID with 8 parallel requests, and it failed on SentencePiece's PDF. GROBID also gives a structured bibliography. The CRF image is pinned by digest and runs natively on Apple silicon.

Why: the bar was "within 2× of GROBID's speed and clearly better"; Docling met only the second half. ADR 0024, ADR 0023.

350-token Chunks, packed by paragraph, counted with a pinned BERT tokenizerADR 0027, 0032

Chunks never cross a Section. The budget leaves room for the context header inside the 512-token limit of candidate embedding models. The tokenizer file is pinned by revision and hash. Each Chunk carries its Edition id beside its pinned id, so v1's Chunks know they belong to the Edition keyed by the latest Version.

Why: one fixed counter makes Chunks reproducible, and the duplicates stage is keyed by Edition. ADR 0027, ADR 0032.

Element rules: tables, captions, references, appendicesADR 0028, 0035, 0043

Tables and captions become their own Chunk kinds; references are kept but not indexed; acknowledgements are dropped; appendices flagged. Missed-table cells stay in text because every cut tried on the Seeds also removed real prose. Lost numbered headings are recovered from the type size in GROBID's coordinates.

Why: the brief asks exactly this question, and numbers that live only in tables must be findable. ADR 0028, ADR 0035, ADR 0043.

A quality gate, per-file failures, and a run that stops when GROBID goes awayADR 0029, 0036

A file fails on a GROBID error, a timeout, fewer than 2 Sections or under 2,000 characters of body. A failed file is recorded and the run goes on. A dropped or refused connection is the service's fault, not the file's, so the run stops instead of marking every remaining file failed.

Why: scanned PDFs are expected and OCR is out of scope; a 2,700-file run must always finish and say what went wrong. ADR 0029, ADR 0036.

Parse only the pinned files of included Editions, from the committed recordADR 0031, 0038, 0030

The parse step reads the full-text record and the manifest, so withdrawn preprints, re-pinned Versions and excluded Editions get no Chunks. The cache is keyed by file hash, parser image and chunking config; changing the parser re-parses, changing the config only re-chunks, and the same inputs give byte-identical output.

Why: the corpus must be exactly the manifest's included rows, and tuning Chunk size later must be cheap. ADR 0031, ADR 0038, ADR 0030.

What could be wrong
  • 585 Chunks in 19 files are invisible base64 from LaTeX figures, 63 are dot leaders, 333 are four words or fewer. Issue 39.
  • A junk heading starting with a letter A–H can open the appendix and flag the rest of the body, in about 7 files. Issue 40.
  • Reference-list text leaks into body Chunks in 4 of 37 sampled files; author lists match every name query. Issue 42.
  • Headless text at the start of the body is filed under the Abstract; 312 Chunks have an empty Section path. Issue 43.
  • About half of all tables are missed and sit as unlabelled numbers in text Chunks. Whether this hurts retrieval is now measurable: 1% of top-5 slots. Issue 26.
  • Korean and Urdu papers parse badly: no headings, reversed script.

Reproduce: just fetch, just grobid-up, just parse. The parse report is data/parse-report.md; the Chunks themselves are not committed.

Stage 4

Retrieve: how a question finds Chunks

done

Rank the 141,348 Chunks for a question. Two baselines exist so every later choice has a number to beat. Every retriever has a Collapsed twin that merges copies of a passage across a Work's files, and a per-paper twin that gives each paper a question names its share of the slots. The embedding comparison has run on both rankings, and bge-small stays: the chatbot retrieves with dense.bge-small+per-paper+collapsed.

Input
A question as text, and the parsed files the parse report lists.
Output
A ranked list of Chunks with scores; a local index cache keyed by chunking config and retriever settings; the manifest's "indexed" mark (2,066 Editions).

Two baselines, both on every Chunk

  • BM25, keyword search: lower-cased letter and digit runs, decimals kept whole, no stemming, no stop words, k1 1.2, b 0.75. Index built in 10 s; a query takes about 200 ms.
  • Dense, vector search: bge-small-en-v1.5 (33M parameters, 384 dimensions), exact dot product over float32 vectors, query prefixed with the model's search instruction. Index built in 14 minutes on the laptop GPU; a query takes about 30 ms.
  • Every retriever indexes the context header with the text.
  • Collapsed ranking (<retriever>+collapsed): takes a retriever's raw top 50 and merges copies of a passage from a Work's other Versions and Editions into one slot, shown from the Canonical Edition's file. Two Chunks are copies when they share at least 40% of their word 5-grams and one is the other's closest Chunk in its file, however the Sections are numbered. A Work's other passages keep their slots, and Surveys are marked, not demoted.
  • Per-paper retrieval (<retriever>+per-paper+collapsed): the answer model lists the papers, methods and models a question names, and any name the question doesn't hold is dropped. "Goldman et al." finds Works by first author, "MEGABYTE" by title words. When two or more names are different Works, their Chunks fill the slots in rotation before the rest of the ranking, and then copies are merged.

The embedding comparison: five more retrievers

  • bge-base-en-v1.5, the size control (110M parameters, 768 dimensions): Recall@5 0.29 raw and 0.32 collapsed, built in 48 minutes.
  • OpenScholar_Retriever, the scientific candidate (Contriever-base trained further on peS2o): Recall@5 0.07 raw and 0.12 collapsed, below bge-small beyond noise on both. It finds the right paper as often as bge-small, but ranks the paper's short Chunks, such as figure captions, above the one holding the Passage.
  • Three hybrids, BM25 plus each dense model by reciprocal rank fusion: Recall@5 0.34, 0.34 and 0.28 raw, 0.39, 0.35 and 0.32 collapsed.
  • Nothing beats bge-small beyond noise on either ranking, so it stays: 0.30 raw and 0.38 collapsed, and 0.43 with per-paper retrieval on top. That last is the retriever [chat] names.

Decisions

The corpus is the parse report's parsed files; the index cache is local and keyedADR 0039

Index and evaluate read only the files the committed parse report lists, not everything in the cache, which also holds Chunks of withdrawn and re-pinned files. The cache folder is named by chunking key and retriever key, so a change to either builds beside the old one. Each file's Chunks cite the report's pinned id and the manifest's Edition, whichever id filled the cache, and the index fingerprint stays over the cached Chunks, so restamping rebuilds nothing.

Why: the thing being scored must be exactly the corpus, and the report checks against the config so a stale cache stops the run. Read ADR 0039, ADR 0066.

Dense baseline behind an embedder boundary, bge-small pinned by revision and weight hashADR 0042

The dense retriever gets vectors only through one function, which tests replace with a hashed bag of words, so the default test suite never loads a model. bge-small shares BERT's vocabulary with the chunker's counter, so Chunks nearly always fit in 512 tokens (5 of 141,360 were cut).

Why: swapping the model for the comparison must touch one place, and tests must stay offline. Read ADR 0042.

Works are grouped in collect; the collapse merges copies, not WorksADR 0051, 0057, 0063

collect joins two Editions into one Work when they share an arXiv id, DOI, ACL ID or file bytes, or a title (hyphens inside words dropped, word order ignored) and a first-author surname: 2,705 included Editions in 2,393 Works. The Collapsed ranking reads that grouping and merges a passage's copies into the best-ranked slot. On the Work groups the rule joins 30 of 30 pairs and nothing more.

Why: one Chunk per Work dropped the passages that answered; merging only copies frees the slots copies took, and a title match with a different author is listed as a Suspected pair, never joined. ADR 0051, ADR 0057, ADR 0063.

Copies are matched by their text, not their place in the SectionADR 0074

The first collapse called two Chunks copies only at the same Section path and the same count of Chunks before it. Real Editions break that four ways: GROBID nests a Section in one Edition only, headings change case, Sections are renumbered, and revisions split paragraphs. Now two Chunks of one Work from different files are copies when they share 40% of their word 5-grams and one is the other's closest Chunk in its file. Near-duplicate chat Turns fell from 15 of 44 to 2, bge-small's copies from another Edition or Version from 33 to 5 of its top-5 slots, and no collapsed Recall@5 dropped.

Why: the failure diagnosis found duplicates still taking slots on the chatbot's retriever. The closest-Chunk condition keeps a table cut into two Chunks from merging with its own other half. ADR 0074.

Retrieve once for each paper a question namesADR 0076

A comparison question becomes one embedding, which lands near whichever paper restates both sides, so the other side goes missing. The answer model now lists the names in the question; each is looked up among the indexed Works by first author or title words, and the named Works' Chunks fill the slots in rotation before the collapse. On the 8 multi-paper questions bge-small's collapsed Recall@5 goes from 0.06 to 0.25; on all 28 answerable from 0.38 to 0.43, with 3 wins and no losses. The alternative, leaning on BM25's match of the name through the hybrid alone, reached 0.10 on the multi-paper questions.

Why: the diagnosis showed MEGABYTE's Passage ranks 1st once its title leads the query and 8th inside a comparison. ADR 0076.

The embedding comparison, raw and collapsed: bge-small staysADR 0065

Seven retrievers over the full corpus, each against bge-small on the 28 answerable questions with paired bootstrap intervals. The best Recall@5, 0.34, belongs to the BM25 + bge-small and BM25 + bge-base hybrids, but their +0.04 lead (interval −0.09 to +0.16) is within noise, so the rule fixed before the run picks the cheaper bge-small. On the Collapsed ranking, which the chatbot will retrieve with, the best is the BM25 + bge-small hybrid's 0.39 against bge-small's 0.38 (+0.01, interval −0.14 to +0.16), so the choice doesn't change. OpenScholar is the only retriever beyond noise on either ranking, and it is a loss.

Why: a rule written before the run keeps the choice from being fitted to the numbers, and one question moves Recall@5 by 0.036. ADR 0065.

Index marks Editions indexed, from a record committed in the SnapshotADR 0045

The index step writes the list of indexed files into the Snapshot and marks the manifest, so the funnel's last row survives a collect rerun and a fresh clone. An Edition whose file failed the gate shows as "with full text, not indexed".

Why: the manifest must show whether each paper made it into the index, and collect rebuilds the manifest from the Snapshot alone. Read ADR 0045.

What could be wrong
  • Asked alone, a follow-up finds its Passage 0% (BM25) and 12% (dense) of the time. The chatbot's Rewrite lifts bge-small's to 0.25 collapsed, still short of the hand-written rewrite's 0.50.
  • Multi-paper questions are still the weakest type: 0.25 with per-paper retrieval. A name the model doesn't list, or a Work whose title lacks the name, gets no slots of its own.
  • More than a third of the collapsed top-5 slots on bge-small still hold another passage of a paper already ranked (47 of 130). They are not copies, so the collapse keeps them on purpose.
  • Exact search over 141k vectors is fine today but is not a production index.

Reproduce: just index builds all seven retrievers (2.5 to 3 hours; --retriever builds one at a time) into data/index-cache/ (not committed). The twins need no index of their own. Settings in config/retrieval.toml.

Stage 5

Evaluate: how retrieval is measured, and why the score can be trusted

done

A labelled set of questions with the quotes that answer them, a scoring rule that is the same every run, and ground truth for duplicates that the system under test never computed.

Input
The Evaluation set, the Work groups, and one or more retrievers.
Output
A committed eval report: Recall@5, MRR@10 and Hit@5 per question type for every retriever and its two twins, paired bootstrap comparisons, the top-5 duplicate rate, Survey restatement, numeric-Chunk share, build time and latency. Rerunning gives the same report apart from timings and the answer model's run-to-run wording.

Nouns born here

Evaluation set
36 questions: 12 single-paper lookups, 8 multi-paper, 8 follow-ups (each with its history and a reference rewrite), 8 unanswerable. About 25 grounded in Seeds, 10 in other papers.
Passage
A verbatim quote of at most 300 characters from one Section of one Edition. Any Chunk holding it answers, whichever Edition or Version it comes from.
Work groups
209 Editions that a retriever or its Collapsed twin put in a top 5, grouped into 179 Works, 3 of them Surveys. The ground truth for the duplicate rate and the grouping rule's test set.

The metrics

  • Recall@5: share of a question's Passages held by a Chunk in the top 5, averaged over questions.
  • MRR@10: 1 over the rank of the first Chunk holding any Passage; 0 if none in the top 10.
  • Duplicate rate: share of top-5 slots holding a Chunk from a Work already ranked higher, and share of questions with at least one such slot. A Survey restating a finding is counted separately as restatement, never as a duplicate.
  • Follow-ups are scored four ways: alone, with history prepended, as a hand-written reference rewrite, and as the chatbot's own Rewrite of the stored history and the follow-up (the rewriter row, skipped without the answer model). The headline row uses "alone", as a user would type it.
  • Unanswerable questions are run but left out of the retrieval metrics; they are for the chatbot's abstention measure.

How the set itself is checked

  • An agent drafts; the developer checks every question. Each row records checked_by; nothing unchecked is scored.

  • The Passage matcher (65% of the quote's words in order, every number equal) was calibrated on 48 v1-versus-latest pairs so a reworded Version still counts.
  • Each unanswerable question has absence evidence: the top 10 Chunks from both retrievers for the question and for probe queries, read and confirmed by the developer.
  • Work groups were drafted from shared identifiers and titles, then checked Work by Work on a local page, or for the 55 Editions added in #54 by a blind agent that grouped them without seeing the draft; each row records who checked it.

Decisions

The Passage matcher, eval-check and the v1/vN calibrationADR 0041

Texts are normalised (NFKC, case, hyphenation, citations dropped), cut into BM25's words, aligned in order; a Chunk holds a Passage at 65% coverage with every number matching. The threshold was set where v1 Chunks still match quotes from the latest Version.

Why: a substring match would miss any reworded Version, and a Version duplicate must count as a wasted slot, not a miss. Read ADR 0041.

Duplicate rate measured against Work groups given as inputADR 0044, 0047

The groups file is committed, drafted from manifest identifiers and matching titles, and checked by a person with a note on each row. 30 of 179 Works have more than one Edition. No Work was merged or split in either check.

Why: a duplicate rate computed by the system being measured would measure nothing. ADR 0044, ADR 0047.

Short quotes may be committed; every question names who checked it; absence is shownADR 0046

Quotes of at most 300 characters are committed as quotation for evaluation whatever the paper's licence, with their source named. A question is unanswerable only after a documented search found nothing in the corpus.

Why: papers stay out of git for licence reasons, but a quote is attribution, not redistribution; and "the corpus can't answer" must be a checked claim. Read ADR 0046.

What could be wrong
  • 28 answerable questions is a small set; a single question moves Recall@5 by 0.036.
  • About 25 of 36 questions are grounded in Seeds, the best-known papers, so the set may flatter retrieval of famous results.
  • The rewriter and per-paper rows ask the answer model, which is not quite deterministic, so those rows can move between runs by a question or two (ADR 0062).
  • Grounding and abstention are measured in Stage 6, not here.

Reproduce: just evaluate after just index. Set: evaluation-set.jsonl, groups: work-groups.csv, absence evidence: absence-evidence.md.

Stage 6

Chat: answering from the papers, or saying they don't answer

partial

A local multi-turn chat page that answers only from retrieved Chunks, cites paper, Edition, Section and pages for every claim, and abstains plainly when the corpus doesn't hold the answer. Built and measured; the final run waits on issue 86, which regenerates it with the fixes of #80–#82 and marks this stage done. #83 and #85 are enhancements, not needed for it.

Input
A typed question and the Conversation before it.
Output
An answer with numbered Citations and a reference list, or the fixed Abstention sentence. A Transcript can be saved from the page. chat-eval asks all 36 questions through the same code and writes the chat report and the answers.

Nouns born here

Conversation
One user's sequence of Turns from the first question until the page is reset.
Turn
One question and its answer, with the Chunks retrieved for it.
Rewrite
The standalone question formed from a follow-up and the Conversation before it; what retrieval sees.
Citation
A marker naming one Chunk retrieved for its Turn. A marker naming any other Chunk is struck through as invalid, never dropped.
Abstention
The fixed sentence "The papers in this corpus do not answer this question.", or a one-sentence decline in the model's own words that cites nothing and goes on to answer nothing.
Grounded
An answer that cites at least one Chunk, every Citation valid, and every number in it found in a cited Chunk.
Claim
A sentence of an answer that cites a Chunk; the unit an LLM grounding judge would score.
Transcript
A Conversation saved from the page as Markdown, rendered from the records the page shows.

What one Turn does

  • Rewrite (follow-ups only): the answer model gets the last 3 Turns and the follow-up, and replaces each word that points back with the paper, method or model it names.
  • Names: the same model lists the papers the question names, for per-paper retrieval.
  • Retrieve: the top 5 Chunks from dense.bge-small+per-paper+collapsed, whatever their scores.
  • Answer: gemma4:e2b-mlx on Ollama, pinned with its runtime, temperature 0, answers from those 5 Chunks only, or abstains by instruction. About 1.5 s per answer on a laptop, and a quarter second more for a follow-up's Rewrite.
  • Show: each reference names the title, Edition and Version, Section and pages, the other Editions whose copies were merged into it ("P16-1162, also 1508.07909v5"), and a Survey mark.

How it is checked

  • chat-eval drives the page's own code. A follow-up asks its stored history first, so the rewriter works from the bot's own answers.
  • --from-answers re-scores the committed replies with the current code, no model needed; the answers file's first line names everything they were asked over and refuses a mismatch.
  • --runs 3 measures the model's run-to-run variation; --baseline marks a verdict that also flipped within the baseline's own runs as noise.
  • A seeded sample of 20 answered Turns is labelled by the developer on a local page (chat-label): is every claim supported by the Chunk it cites?
  • A failure diagnosis traced each kind of failure in those labels to where it first goes wrong, and filed #80–#85: 2026-10-21 diagnosis.

Decisions

A pinned local answer model behind a one-method boundary, and a local pageADR 0054

gemma4:e2b-mlx (5.2B, Apache 2.0) on Ollama, pinned by digest and runtime version as the judge is, with thinking off, temperature 0 and seed 0. Tests swap in a scripted fake, so none needs Ollama.

Why: a reviewer reruns it with no API key, and the pin makes "the same model" checkable. Read ADR 0054.

Follow-ups are rewritten before retrieval, and the Rewrite names the paperADR 0056, 0067, 0073

The answer model with its own prompt rewrites each follow-up from the last 3 Turns, earlier answers shown without their markers. The prompt now asks that each pointing word be replaced by the paper, method or model as the Conversation names it. evaluate scores the chatbot's Rewrite as a fourth row beside the hand-written one.

Why: asked alone, "how does it …" names nothing; the first prompt replaced "it" with "the model", which retrieves nothing better. ADR 0056, ADR 0067, ADR 0073.

Abstention by instruction, with a wider reading of the model's own declinesADR 0059, 0064

Every Turn sends its 5 Chunks whatever their scores, and the system prompt says to reply with the fixed sentence when they don't answer. No score threshold or second model decides. A one-sentence decline such as "The provided chunks do not contain …" also counts, unless it goes on to answer or cites a Chunk.

Why: the model often declined in its own words, and those were being scored as answers. The false-abstention rate is reported beside the abstention rate, so a bot that abstains on everything shows. ADR 0059, ADR 0064.

Re-scoring from the answers, run-to-run variation, Wilson intervalsADR 0062

The model is not quite deterministic at temperature 0: one run changed the wording of 14 of 44 replies. Rather than force it, scoring is made exactly reproducible from the stored replies, variation is measured over three runs, and every rate carries an interval.

Why: with 8 unanswerable questions one verdict moves the rate by 0.12, far more than the model's noise. ADR 0062.

The chatbot retrieves collapsed, and citations name the merged EditionsADR 0069, 0070, 0076

[chat] names a twin, so the 5 Chunks are 5 different passages. A reference reads "2112.06598v2, also v1" when copies were merged, and marks a Survey. ADR 0076 adds per-paper retrieval on top.

Why: copies of one passage were taking slots a comparison needed for its other paper. ADR 0069, ADR 0070, ADR 0076.

The Transcript is saved from the page, not written by handADR 0068

A save button renders the Conversation's Turn records to Markdown in docs/reports, with the question, Rewrite, answer, references, invalid markers and uncited Chunks. The committed one has six Turns: a follow-up whose "it" resolves to CANINE, and three Abstentions, one on an off-topic question. It is not edited.

Why: the brief asks for a real conversation, and one renderer for page and file keeps them saying the same thing. ADR 0068.

The grounding verdict checked by hand, and an LLM judge measured, not adoptedADR 0071, 0072, 0075

chat-eval --export-check samples 20 answered Turns; the developer labels each on a local page; the report gives the agreement and every disagreement with its note, scored on the sampled reply itself so a later run doesn't drop labels. --judge has Tev1 give P(yes) for each Claim and cited Chunk, threshold 0.5 set before looking.

Why: a check that reads only markers and numbers can't see a borrowed explanation. Tev1 caught none of the three misses either, so the grounded rate stays the automatic check's. ADR 0071, ADR 0072, ADR 0075.

What could be wrong
  • The committed run predates the text collapse, per-paper retrieval and the new rewrite prompt, so its numbers are not yet the chatbot's as built. Issue 86.
  • The grounded rate overstates grounding: the check passes misattributed and borrowed claims, as 3 of 19 hand labels show.
  • The answer model can attach one paper's claim to another in a comparison. Issue 83.
  • A 5B model answers some near-miss questions it should abstain on, and gives incomplete answers; a larger one is untested. Issue 85.

Reproduce: just chat serves the page; just chat-eval asks the set (Ollama with the pinned model), just chat-eval --from-answers re-scores the committed replies without it.

Worked example

One paper through every stage, and one question traced back

SentencePiece (Kudo and Richardson, 2018) is a Seed with two Editions, so it exercises every noun on this page.

Forwards: SentencePiece, the Work

Scopestage 0

Topic "subword algorithms". Picked as Seed sentencepiece: "the standard language-independent tokenizer library; cited by most tokenizer work since 2018".

Discoverstage 1

Found by 20 Discovery channels: three arXiv queries, the OpenAlex query, three Semantic Scholar queries, the Seed itself, two Seeds' citation lists and eleven Seeds' reference lists. Two Editions: the arXiv preprint 1808.06226v1 (one Version, arXiv's non-exclusive licence) and the EMNLP demo paper D18-2012 (CC BY 4.0), kept as separate rows.

Judgestage 2

Rules: pass (title contains "tokenizer" and "subword"). Judge: P(Yes) 0.9971 ≥ 0.8. Verdict: included, stage judge, on both Editions.

Acquire & parsestage 3

Two pinned files, 1808.06226v1.pdf (sha256 8a81eb…) and D18-2012.pdf (cf056a…). Both parsed: 12 Sections each; 24 Chunks (1 table, 3 captions) and 26 Chunks (0 tables, 4 captions). Docling had failed on this PDF in the spike; GROBID did not.

Retrievestage 4

Both Editions indexed, so a SentencePiece query can return the same sentence twice. collect puts them in one Work, so the Collapsed ranking shows one slot for both, read from the Canonical venue Edition and labelled "D18-2012 + 1808.06226v1".

Evaluatestage 5

Question sentencepiece-whitespace asks what the library does with spaces. Its Passage, checked by the developer, is in 1808.06226v1 § 3.1 Lossless Tokenization:

SentencePiece first escapes the whitespace with a meta symbol _ (U+2581)

BM25 does not find it in the top 10. Dense finds it at rank 4 with a duplicate slot in its top 5; on the Collapsed ranking, at rank 3 with none.

Backwards: two questions and what they test

Follow-upcanine-hashing

History: "What score does CANINE reach on Amharic named entities, a language it never saw in pre-training, and why can it read that script at all?" Follow-up: "How does it give every possible character an embedding without an enormous table?" Rewrite: "How does CANINE embed every Unicode codepoint without a vocabulary-sized embedding matrix?"

Passage in 2103.06874v4 § 3.1 Model. Asked alone, neither retriever finds it: "it" names nothing. With the reference rewrite, BM25 finds it at rank 2 and dense at rank 1. The chatbot's own Rewrite, "How does CANINE give every possible character an embedding without an enormous table?", also puts it at rank 1 for dense, and it is Turn 2 of the committed Transcript.

Unanswerablebpe-english-french

"What BLEU score did Sennrich et al.'s subword NMT paper report for English→French translation?" The paper reports English→German and English→Russian only. The absence evidence lists the top 10 Chunks from both retrievers for the question and for probe queries; the first hits are the paper's own English→German results and other systems' French scores. The developer confirmed none answers it. The chatbot must say so rather than quote the German figure; on the committed run it abstains.

Why tracein this order

Reading the vocabulary forwards shows how a record becomes a citable Chunk. Reading a question backwards shows what the score actually rewards: a quote found in any copy of the paper, counted once.
