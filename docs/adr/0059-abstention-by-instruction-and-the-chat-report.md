# 0059. Abstention by instruction, and the chat report that measures it

- **Date:** 2026-10-19
- **Status:** Accepted

## Context
Spec #63 asks the bot to say plainly when the corpus doesn't support an answer, and the write-up to measure how often answers are grounded and how the unanswerable questions are handled. The Evaluation set has 8 unanswerable questions out of 36. Six are near misses: the corpus holds the paper or the measure, just not this answer (Sennrich et al.'s English→French BLEU, the Byte Latent Transformer at 70B). Two are out of topic (vitamin D, the 2018 World Cup). ADR 0054 already has the model write a fixed Abstention sentence, and its smoke run found a near miss answered from an unrelated Chunk instead. Issue #67 is the slice that measures this: a `chat-eval` command, a chat report and the answers, and this record of how the bot abstains.

## Decision
**The bot abstains by instruction, with the retrieved Chunks in front of it.** Every Turn retrieves its k Chunks and sends them to the answer model, whatever their scores. Rule 4 of the system prompt tells it to reply with the fixed Abstention sentence, and nothing else, when the Chunks don't answer. A reply holding the sentence, compared after the Passage matcher's normalisation, is an Abstention (0054). No retrieval score decides it, and no second model judges it.

**`chat-eval` measures it on the Evaluation set, through the page's own code.** It drives the same `Chatbot` as the page, resetting it between questions. Each single-paper, multi-paper and unanswerable question is a Conversation of one Turn. A follow-up asks its stored history as real Turns, the bot answering each, then the follow-up, so the rewriter works from the bot's own answers. Only a question's own Turn is scored; its history's Turns are in the answers file and the latency. It refuses a set that fails `eval-check`, through the same gate as `evaluate`, and checks the answer model's pin before asking anything.

**Each unanswerable question says how far it is from the corpus.** `unanswerable_kind` is `near-miss` or `out-of-topic`, and `eval-check` fails an unanswerable question without one, or an answerable one with any. The abstention rate is reported on all eight and on each kind, and the false-abstention rate on the 28 answerable questions beside it, so a bot that abstains on everything shows.

**The grounding check is a function of the Turn.** An answer that is not an Abstention is grounded when it cites at least one retrieved Chunk, has no invalid marker, and every number in it appears in a cited Chunk. Numbers are the words, cut as the Passage matcher cuts them, that start with a digit: "50k", "0.1", "32,000", "5.3". A name holding a digit, such as ByT5, mT5 or F1, is not a number. Counting it would fail an answer whose Chunk spells "F 1" as GROBID left it. A cited Chunk is its context header and its text, as the model was shown it, so "Section 5.3" is found in the header. An uncited answer is not grounded, whatever it says. An Abstention is neither grounded nor not, and is left out of the grounded rate, a false one included.

**The chat report and the answers.** The report (`data/eval/chat-report.md`) names the Evaluation set's sha256, the corpus, the retriever and k, the answer model's pin and settings, and the sha256 of the system prompt (with the Abstention sentence filled in) and of the rewrite prompt. It gives, per question type and overall, the abstention rate, the grounded rate and Recall@k of the Chunks each Turn retrieved, then a line per question, then p50 and p95 latency per Turn for retrieval, rewriting (over the Turns that were rewritten) and answering. The timings come last, so two runs differ only there. The answers (`data/eval/chat-answers.jsonl`) hold every Turn, history included, with its raw reply, its retrieved and cited Chunk ids and its verdicts, and no timings, so a rerun writes the same file.

## Alternatives considered
- **A retrieval-score threshold**: abstain when the best Chunk scores below a cut. It costs no model call. But the threshold would be tuned on the eight questions that test it, so the rate would measure the tuning. The dense scores are cosines whose scale moves with the query's length and wording, and BM25's are not comparable across queries at all. A near miss is exactly the case where the corpus holds Chunks that score well and still don't answer. The spec rules it out unless the measured rates make the case.
- **A judge model deciding whether the Chunks answer**, such as the pinned Tev1 asked "do these Chunks answer this question?". It is a second model call on every Turn and a second pin at chat time, and the report would then measure two models. #70 adds Tev1 as a grounding judge, measured against the hand labels, which is where a model judging the model belongs.
- **Counting every word holding a digit as a number**, as the Passage matcher does for quotes. In a quote a changed digit is a different sentence. In an answer, model names are the commonest words with digits, and the check would fail correct answers for naming ByT5. A spelled-out number ("ten") is still not checked: the matcher's normalisation doesn't map it to digits either. Nor are "32k" and "32,000" one number: the matcher keeps them as different words, so an answer that rounds a Chunk's figure into another notation is not grounded. The check errs strict, as the matcher does.
- **Checking numbers against every retrieved Chunk rather than the cited ones.** It would pass an answer that copies a number from Chunk 3 and cites Chunk 1, which is the misattribution the check is for.
- **Scoring a follow-up's history Turns too.** They have no Passages and were written to set up the follow-up, so they would add Turns to the grounded rate that no question asks about.

## Consequences
- The committed run (dense.bge-small, gemma4:e2b-mlx at its pin, 36 questions in 44 Turns, about 2.5 minutes from cold on an M1 Max) gives: no false Abstention on the 28 answerable questions; the out-of-topic questions abstained 2/2 and the near misses 0/6; 27 of the 34 answers that are not Abstentions grounded (0.79), 25 of 28 on the answerable questions; Recall@5 of the Chunks the Turns retrieved 0.41 overall, 0.06 on multi-paper. Answering is p50 0.45 s and p95 0.82 s per Turn once the model is loaded.
- **The model declines in its own words.** Five of the six near misses, and two answerable questions whose Chunks missed (llama2-70b-vocabulary, superbpe-mmlu), were answered "The provided chunks do not contain …" instead of the fixed sentence. They are counted as answers, not Abstentions, and as not grounded, because they cite nothing; byt5-learning-rate counts as grounded, because its decline carries two stray markers. So the 0/6 near-miss rate measures the fixed phrase as much as the decision: the bot mostly declines the near misses but not in the words the report counts. Either the prompt must hold the model to the sentence, or the Abstention match must widen. That is a change to what an Abstention is (story 37), left to a follow-up so it is measured against this run.
- **The pinned model is not run-to-run deterministic.** Three runs on one index retrieved the same Chunks and gave the same Abstentions, but 14 of the 44 replies changed wording despite temperature 0 and seed 0, and one grounded verdict flipped (gpt-4-number-tokens), so the grounded rate was 27 or 28 of 34. The report is reproducible apart from timings only for a deterministic model, as the tests' fake is; with the real one, expect a verdict or two to move between runs. 0056's opt-in test, which asks one question twice in a row, did not catch it.
- The matcher's hyphen rule joins "8B-parameter" into one word, "8bparameter", which no Chunk spelling "8B parameters" holds. The check reports it as a number missing from the cited Chunks, which is visible in the per-question line.
